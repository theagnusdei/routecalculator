## Texto extraído

GXO Logistics plans to accelerate deployments of adaptive technologies across the U.S. in 2027. The phased rollout will start at GXO’s priority sites and continue over the next 12 months.
The deployment will include:
- Pallet AMRs to automate repetitive material transport and improve warehouse and production flow.
- Robotic arms deployable across multiple applications including inbound unloading and packing solutions.
- AI-enabled vision technologies to enable hands-free data capture, improving productivity and inventory checks.
- Flexible and scalable automated sortation systems.
“Today’s supply chains require solutions that are efficient and adaptable,” said Michael Jacobs, CEO of the Americas & Asia Pacific at GXO. “This deployment underscores our commitment to helping customers modernize their operations with technologies that can deliver immediate value while positioning them for long-term growth. By leveraging advanced automation and vision-enabled solutions across multiple industries, we are enabling safer, smarter and more responsive operations across our footprint.”
GXO said it expects the deployments to help sites increase efficiency, optimize labor resources, and enhance service levels. The company didn’t disclose which providers it would be sourcing these robots from, but let’s take a look at the existing technologies in its portfolio.
GXO continues investing in humanoids
Earlier this month, GXO shared it is expanding its partnership with Apptronik. The partners are working toward a global rollout of up to 1,000 Apollo humanoids over the next four years. Jeff Cardenas, co-founder and CEO of Apptronik, said in an interview with investment firm RoboStrategy that, when the company started working with GXO in 2024, it tested a range of tasks to see what Apollo would be best at. Eventually, the companies landed on logistics tasks, like kitting.
“We measured the performance of the model against a benchmark, which is basically a human benchmark, and we showed that the model could perform 96% efficient for those tasks,” Cardenas said.
Under the agreement, Apptronik said that it and GXO will expand joint trials of Apollo in live operations across a range of warehouse workflows. These include goods-to-person (G2P) and person-to-goods picking, case and tote movement, packing, and sortation.
“Where humanoids are at today is we’re proving out the KPIs,” Cardenas said. “Can we actually do these tasks with an ROI that makes sense? Then, once you do that, you really get into site acceptance testing, where you can start to go from a handful of robots to the 1,000 units that we’re excited to deploy.”
This fall, GXO also expanded its deployments with Agility Robotics, another humanoid developer. GXO said it plans to deploy Agility’s Digit humanoids in its North Carolina and Tennessee sites. GXO first deployed Digit in late 2023 at a facility in Flowery Branch, Ga. Since then, Digit has moved more than 100,000 totes at the Flowery Branch facility, demonstrating its ability to perform repetitive material handling tasks in an active logistics environment.
GXO is also testing Reflex from Reflex Robotics, but hasn’t given an update on if it plans to expand those tests.
In the company’s second-quarter earnings call in August, GXO CEO Patrick Kelleher acknowledged humanoids had not yet produced a return on investment (ROI).
What other technology providers does GXO work with?
Outside of humanoids, GXO has deployed a range of other kinds of automation in its facilities. For example, the company has deployed Exotec Skypod system at its facility supporting global fashion brand Guess. Skypod is an automated storage and retrieval system (ASRS) that unified picking and packing.
Earlier this year, GXO deployed the Autoload system in Europe for Grupa Żywiec in Elbląg, one of the leading beer producers. GXO said the technology increases throughput, enhances workplace safety and elevates operational standards. The system automated trailer loading and unloading, replacing traditional forklift operations.
In 2025, the company deployed Dexory’s automatic inventory robot at its site in Oosterhout, Netherlands. It also had plans to expand deployments to the U.S. and the rest of Europe. DexoryView automates manual cycle counts and stock checks, which it said can help its customers save over 70% of labor time.
