Solar-powered radiation detector for use in disaster zones
A portable, solar-powered radiation detector is described in the International Journal of Energy Technology and Policy. The device was designed to operate continuously without utility power, potentially improving radiation monitoring in remote areas and disaster zones when and where an external power supply is unavailable.
The system combines solar panels with rechargeable lithium batteries, drawing power from sunlight while storing energy for periods of low illumination or power loss. Tests demonstrated 24 hours of uninterrupted operation under illumination of 200 lux, a relatively low light level. The device also supports USB fast charging for times when there is access to power.
The detector uses a Geiger-Müller (GM) tube, a sensor that detects ionizing radiation, including gamma and beta radiation. It uses a dynamic threshold algorithm to adjust the level at which an alarm is triggered, reducing false alarms by 30%, according to the researchers.
This novel device addresses a limitation of conventional portable detectors, which usually need an external electricity supply or have restricted battery life. Continuous operation during power failures could prove particularly valuable following earthquakes and nuclear accidents, when infrastructure may be damaged. Fundamentally, the device makes radiation monitoring accessible and less dependent on established infrastructure.
The researchers argue that the relatively simple design, using established electronic components, should allow affordable mass production, which could open up a putative market for a home device that one might add to a household emergency kit along with one's clockwork radio, rechargeable flashlight, battery packs and other equipment one might need in a future emergency situation.
