## Texto extraído

Jensen Huang is back at Nvidia, just 11 days after resigning amid revelations about extramarital affairs and concerns about a possible foreign intelligence connection. His return sent Nvidia’s stock soaring in early trading, and President Donald Trump says he personally helped convince the company’s board to reinstate him.
That’s quite a turnaround for a CEO whose return the board had publicly ruled out just last week.
Nvidia’s directors say their independent investigation will continue, even as Huang slips back into his trademark leather jacket. The leadership crisis may be over, but the questions surrounding his departure aren’t. Meanwhile, Nvidia’s enormously lucrative AI business rolls on. There’s a reason so many powerful people wanted Huang back.
When Huang stepped down, I wrote about how Nvidia’s AI business had become so staggeringly lucrative that even a new CEO wouldn’t be likely to put GeForce gamers first. I also explained why Nvidia couldn’t simply flip a switch and start pumping out more graphics cards even if it wanted to.
The supply chain is complicated. The economics aren’t.
Nvidia’s GPUs have become essential to the AI infrastructure boom, turning the company into a financial powerhouse whose fortunes can move the broader stock market. Investors were rattled by Huang’s departure. When Nvidia announced his return Monday morning, shares jumped more than 30 percent in early trading.
Wall Street’s enthusiasm makes sense. Few executives are as closely associated with their company’s success as Huang. But when the president of the United States gets involved? That’s something else entirely.
Now you’re playing with power!
President Donald Trump told CNBC that he’d personally spoken with Nvidia’s board about reinstating Huang.

“I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” Trump said. “And I convinced them.”
Asked whether the stock market’s reaction influenced his thinking, Trump acknowledged, “Probably so,” before emphasizing the longer-term stakes. His explanation? Huang has “other chips in his brain that nobody else knows about.”
The president of the United States personally lobbied to restore the embattled CEO of one of America’s most consequential technology companies. Nvidia’s board confirmed receiving input from senior government officials.
And then think about how far Nvidia has come. Huang helped transform a company best known for GeForce graphics cards into a cornerstone of the global AI economy. Nvidia’s chips are central to the ambitions of technology giants, investors, and governments alike. Nvidia spent decades disrupting computing. Now the people who depend on its success have a powerful interest in keeping its leadership stable.
Adam Patrick Murray / IDG
And there’s a legitimate case for Huang’s comeback. I’ve spent a lot of my career marveling at Nvidia’s technological wizardry, and both his vision and execution deserve enormous credit. Nvidia says government assurances satisfied the board that his reinstatement poses no national security risk. The continuing investigation, however, means serious questions remain unresolved. Returning to the CEO’s chair isn’t the same thing as answering them.
Still, there’s something deeply unsettling about watching Wall Street and the White House rally around one executive before the investigation has even concluded.
For GeForce gamers, the business calculation hasn’t changed either. Nvidia continues to create incredible graphics technology, but AI brings in vastly more money. Huang’s departure wasn’t going to reverse those priorities, and his return certainly won’t.
Nvidia built its empire by changing the rules of computing. Now it’s become so important to Wall Street and Washington that changing who’s in charge is, apparently, a much harder sell.
