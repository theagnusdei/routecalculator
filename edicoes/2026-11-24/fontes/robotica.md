## Texto extraído

So much for robotics being in its post-Jensen Huang era. Just 11 days after his resignation amid allegations of personal misconduct and concerns about potential national security risks involving China, NVIDIA has reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO.
The company announced today that its board of directors unanimously voted to bring Huang back. Huang also returns to NVIDIA’s board, effective immediately.
NVIDIA said the board was satisfied with Huang’s candor and ability to lead the company. It also consulted with senior U.S. government officials before determining that his reinstatement poses no national security risk.
“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers,” said Harvey Jones, NVIDIA’s board chair.
The decision marks a dramatic reversal for NVIDIA. During the company’s earnings call on Nov. 17, Jones explicitly ruled out bringing Huang back, saying the board had unanimously accepted his resignation and did not intend to reinstate him. Just six days later, the board unanimously voted to restore Huang to the position he had held since co-founding NVIDIA in 1993.
The reversal followed growing pressure from President Donald Trump, NVIDIA employees, investors and prominent technology executives. Trump publicly supported Huang’s potential return on Nov. 16, saying he would not object to his reinstatement. On Sunday, Nov. 22, Trump again urged NVIDIA’s board to bring Huang back.
Meanwhile, a “Stand With Jensen” petition attracted more than 200,000 signatures, including support from current and former NVIDIA employees, investors and technology leaders such as Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney.
NVIDIA also faced considerable financial uncertainty following Huang’s departure, with its market value falling sharply and questions emerging about who could replace an executive who had led the company for more than three decades.
It remains unclear precisely what persuaded the board to reverse course. NVIDIA did not identify which government officials were consulted or disclose what information they provided. The company also did not say whether the public campaign influenced its decision.
Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO following Huang’s resignation, will continue in her role as chief financial officer. Jones will remain board chair.
“I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a new industrial revolution,” Huang said. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.”
Huang also thanked NVIDIA’s board, employees, shareholders and his family for their support, adding, “I can’t wait to get back to work.”
National security investigation continues
Huang’s reinstatement does not end the scrutiny surrounding his departure. NVIDIA said an independent board committee will continue investigating reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort.
The committee has retained independent counsel, and Huang has committed to cooperating fully with the investigation. NVIDIA also recently established a National Security and Export Control Committee composed entirely of independent directors. The committee will oversee trade compliance and national security risks, with support from legal counsel and national security specialists.
The company did not provide a timeline for completing its internal investigation.
Huang’s return brings back robotics’ biggest champion
Huang’s return also restores one of the robotics industry’s most visible advocates to the helm of a company that has become increasingly important to robot development.
As The Robot Report previously examined, Huang has spent years promoting robotics and physical AI as major long-term opportunities. He has regularly featured robots in NVIDIA’s keynote presentations and helped bring greater attention to the industry’s potential.
Under his leadership, NVIDIA expanded its robotics portfolio with technologies including Isaac Sim, Isaac Lab, GR00T foundation models, Isaac ROS and Jetson computing platforms.
NVIDIA has also invested in and partnered with robotics developers, including humanoid companies, as it works to establish its technology as a foundation for robot training, simulation and deployment.
