## Texto extraído

Striped semiconductor panel improves hydrogen production by separating water-splitting reaction sites
Scientists from the Institute of Metal Research (IMR) of the Chinese Academy of Sciences have developed an ordered patterning strategy that integrates p-type and n-type BiVO₄ stripes on a conductive substrate. This strategy enables efficient Z-scheme charge separation and significantly enhances the photocatalytic overall water-splitting process. The research is published in the journal Advanced Energy Materials.
Although solar-driven water splitting is a promising route to green hydrogen, photogenerated electrons and holes tend to recombine, and surface reverse reactions severely limit efficiency. Solid-state Z-scheme heterojunctions connect two semiconductors through a conductive mediator and can provide a high driving force for water splitting while directing high-energy electrons and holes to different components. However, conventional particle-based Z-scheme systems rely on random interparticle contacts, which makes charge-transfer pathways uncontrollable and often exacerbates recombination.
Ordered stripes direct charge transfer
To address this issue, the researchers proposed an ordered patterning strategy. Using photolithography, they fabricated alternating stripes of p-type tetragonal zircon BiVO₄ (t-BVO) and n-type monoclinic scheelite BiVO₄ (m-BVO) on an FTO conductive glass substrate.
The FTO layer serves as a conductive mediator that establishes Z-scheme charge-transfer channels between adjacent stripes. Photogenerated electrons accumulate on the p-type t-BVO stripes and photogenerated holes accumulate on the n-type m-BVO stripes. This spatial separation effectively suppresses charge recombination and reverse reactions.
The patterned panel was fabricated by first depositing an m-BVO film on FTO via metal-organic decomposition, then patterning it by photolithography and wet etching, and finally hydrothermally growing t-BVO epitaxially on the exposed FTO regions. The resulting t-BVO//m-BVO alternating stripes were approximately 15 μm wide. Photochemical deposition, surface photovoltage imaging, Kelvin probe force microscopy and surface photovoltage measurements confirm the Z-scheme charge-transfer behavior.
Separated reaction sites boost hydrogen yields
The researchers then selectively photodeposited Rh@CrOₓ as a hydrogen-evolution cocatalyst on the t-BVO stripes and MnOₓ as an oxygen-evolution cocatalyst on the m-BVO stripes. This spatially separated the oxidation and reduction sites and further suppressed charge recombination and reverse reactions.
Under full-spectrum illumination, the patterned panel achieves stoichiometric overall water splitting, producing 1.77 mmol m⁻² of hydrogen and 0.84 mmol m⁻² of oxygen within one hour, with yields about four times higher than those of the unpatterned control.
This work demonstrates that combining ordered semiconductor patterning with a conductive substrate–mediated Z-scheme is an effective strategy for improving the efficiency of photocatalytic overall water splitting. This approach provides a new method for designing scalable photocatalytic panels.
