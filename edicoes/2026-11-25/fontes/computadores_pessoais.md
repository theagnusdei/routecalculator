## Texto extraído

Winners & losers: Nvidia's board has unanimously reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as the company's president, CEO, and board director. The reappointment comes 11 days after Huang resigned following revelations of multiple extramarital affairs, including one with a Singapore-based businesswoman whom US intelligence suspects of being a Chinese spy.
The move marks a sharp reversal by the board. Chair Harvey C. Jones said last week that the company had no plans to reinstate Huang as CEO, a statement that came after Huang said he would be "honored" to return if the board wanted him back.

In an official statement, Nvidia said the decision followed input from senior US officials, who reportedly confirmed that his return posed no national security risk. The company said the board was confident in Huang's ability to lead, and that his reinstatement "is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers."
 – Trung Phan (@TrungTPhan) November 23, 2026
Nvidia said its investigation into the incident is continuing and that Huang's reappointment will not affect the process. "Mr. Huang fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it," the company said, adding that it will continue to cooperate with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.
Nvidia's statement did not name any US official or elected politician, but the decision came a day after President Trump called for Huang's return. Trump posted on Truth Social that he hopes the Nvidia board will rehire Jensen Huang. "It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A.," he added.
In a statement following the announcement, Huang said he was honored to return to Nvidia and was taking on the responsibility "with the greatest humility and renewed commitment." He also thanked the board for believing in him and reinstating him as the company's president and chief executive.
Nvidia shares, which had slumped almost 50% from their October peak, surged 29% on news of Huang's reinstatement. The stock was trading up nearly 34% this morning from last Friday's 52-week low of $124.50. It's still trading about 31% below its all-time high of $243.37 from October 2026.

Huang resigned from Nvidia in disgrace days after The New York Times reported that he was "socializing" with Alice Huang, who US intelligence officials suspected of spying for Beijing. The relationship was subsequently confirmed as romantic by Financial Times before reports of at least two more affairs with Nvidia employees became public.
