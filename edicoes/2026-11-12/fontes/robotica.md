## Texto extraído

Fifteen years of research, technology development, and professional training have helped transform robotics in Brazil. This journey was celebrated by USP’s Center for Robotics (CRob), in São Carlos, during an anniversary event that brought together researchers and members of the academic community to look back on its achievements, present projects, and discuss the institution’s next steps. The event took place on October 14 at the Institute of Mathematics and Computer Science (ICMC).
Established in 2011 through a joint initiative by the Institute of Mathematics and Computer Science (ICMC) and the São Carlos School of Engineering (EESC), CRob has become one of Brazil’s leading centers for research and development in the field. The center currently brings together researchers from different laboratories and maintains partnerships with universities, research institutions, and companies in Brazil and abroad.
During the celebration, representatives of laboratories affiliated with the center presented some of the research and technologies developed in recent years. Members of the management committee led the presentations, giving attendees an overview of CRob’s diverse areas of activity, which range from industrial robotics to autonomous systems and applications across different sectors of society.
The meeting also marked the release of a technical report outlining the center’s activities and achievements over its 15-year history. The report is available online.
For Fernando Santos Osório, an ICMC professor, CRob member, and one of the event’s organizers, the anniversary marks the maturation of a project built collectively by researchers who believed in the potential of robotics. “The Robotics Center’s 15-year history reflects a successful journey, with significant achievements, major challenges, and important results. Above all, it demonstrates how much the center has matured”, he said.
According to Osório, the work of the 10 professors who served on the management committee and devoted themselves to consolidating the initiative was essential to CRob’s current standing. “We are very pleased to see how far we have come, and even more pleased to know that we have a very promising path ahead.”
From research to applications in society
CRob’s importance is closely tied to the development of technologies that make a wide range of activities more efficient, safer, and more automated. In industry, agribusiness, and the service sector, robotic systems can perform repetitive, physically demanding, or dangerous tasks while also improving productivity and process quality.
In this context, the center plays a strategic role by bringing together expertise from different fields, particularly computer science and engineering. Collaboration between researchers at the ICMC and EESC enables the development of solutions that combine intelligent systems, sensors, control mechanisms, and automation technologies.
Osório noted that this collaboration has become even more important in light of advances in artificial intelligence (AI), an area in which the ICMC has established expertise and which is increasingly used to develop robots capable of perceiving their surroundings, learning, and making decisions.
The center’s location in São Carlos is also considered an advantage. The city is home to numerous universities, researchers, and technology-based companies, creating favorable conditions for partnerships and the development of innovative solutions.
For the professor, CRob is not only an achievement for USP but also an important means of strengthening the São Carlos campus’s position as a leading center for robotics research and innovation, while expanding opportunities for collaboration with institutions in Brazil and abroad.
.
New building marks the beginning of a new phase
Along with celebrating the achievements of the past 15 years, the anniversary comes at a time when the center’s infrastructure is expanding. In January 2026, USP held a groundbreaking ceremony for CRob’s new facility, which will be built in Area 2 of the São Carlos campus.
With an estimated investment of approximately R$ 64.7 million, the project calls for a three-story building with an area of about 6,500 square meters. The facility will house specialized laboratories, prototyping spaces, areas for testing robotic systems, and spaces for collaborative research.
For Osório, the new facility fulfills a long-held aspiration shared by the center’s members. “The new CRob building at USP’s Area 2 campus in São Carlos represents the realization of a dream”, he said.
The centralization of groups and laboratories, which are currently spread across different locations, is expected to facilitate equipment sharing, strengthen collaboration among teams, and enable more complex projects. According to the researcher, the new infrastructure should also create more opportunities for cooperation with external institutions, reinforcing the center’s role as a leading developer of robotic technologies.
.
Tribute recognizes the contribution of one of the founders
One of the highlights of the celebration was a tribute to Marco Henrique Terra, in recognition of his fundamental role in the creation and development of CRob. Terra was one of the key people responsible for bringing together the researchers who launched the project and became the center’s first general coordinator. His work was decisive in establishing the initiative as a USP Research Support Center, bringing together different areas of scientific expertise to advance robotics.
During the ceremony, Terra received a plaque recognizing his academic career, scientific contributions, and dedication to strengthening the center. The tribute also underscored the collective nature of a history built over 15 years through collaboration among professors, students, and researchers from different specialties.
Looking back on past achievements and ahead to the opportunities created by the new facility, the 15th-anniversary celebration reaffirmed the importance of continuing research and training new generations of professionals.
For Osório, the occasion offered an opportunity not only to recognize what has already been achieved but also to consider the possibilities arising from the institution’s growth. What began as a collaboration among researchers from the ICMC and EESC is now preparing to enter a new chapter in robotics research at USP.
