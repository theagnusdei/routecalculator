## Texto extraído

In context: Most gaming PCs and handhelds sold today don't come with a disc drive. So for anyone with a shelf of original PlayStation and Sega Saturn games, playing them on a modern machine usually ends with a trip to a ROM site. Playmaji's Polymega Remix was built to offer a legal way around that. The company now says new orders should arrive in time for the holidays.
The Remix first went up for pre-order in April for $199. Playmaji raised the price to $229 in October – saying costs for nearly everything kept climbing – and reopened orders with holiday shipping at the same time. Its product page now promises orders will ship by the end of the year.
It's also finally going on sale in Japan. Pre-orders there opened on November 17 for 48,400 yen (including tax), or roughly $300, with shipments planned for late December., or roughly $300, with shipments planned for late December.
The catch is that, for all its console looks, the Remix can't play a single game on its own. It has no HDMI port, storage, or Wi-Fi. Instead, it plugs into a Windows 11 PC over USB and lets that machine do the emulation through the free Polymega App.
Its slot-loading drive reads PlayStation, Saturn, Sega CD, Neo Geo CD and TurboGrafx-CD discs from Japan, North America and Europe. Owners can play games straight from the disc or install them on their PC. Once installed, a game keeps working even after the Remix is unplugged, so a handheld like the ROG Ally doesn't need the drive dangling off its side on the go.
Saturn games are the hardest to run, though. Playmaji recommends a processor with around 3GHz of single-core performance for them, and the app wants at least 8GB of RAM in any case. It also still needs an internet connection for its game database, with offline play promised in a later update.
According to Generation Amiga's rundown of the latest app update, Steam Deck owners may run into problems with the drive. Some AMD systems can also experience intermittent audio glitches.
For cartridge games, Playmaji sells add-on Element Modules that bring NES, SNES, Genesis, TurboGrafx-16 and N64 support. Most cost $99 each with a matching controller in the box. N64 games only started running in the Windows app in September.
Meanwhile, PC players are getting more ways to use old discs without the original hardware. RPCS3 recently started booting PS3 games straight from Blu-ray, and custom firmware can now turn some PC Blu-ray drives into GameCube and Wii rippers.
