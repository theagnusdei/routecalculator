## Texto extraído

Rockwell Automation has signed a definitive agreement to acquire AGILOX, an Austria-based developer of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs). Rockwell said the acquisition will more than double the size of its AMR business and complement its existing OTTO Motors portfolio.
Financial terms of the acquisition were not disclosed. The deal is expected to close in the second quarter of Rockwell’s fiscal 2027, subject to regulatory approval. AGILOX will become part of Rockwell’s Intelligent Devices operating segment.
AGILOX said it has deployed more than 2,500 AMRs worldwide since its founding in 2017. The company has more than 250 employees and operations in Europe, North America, and China. Its robots are used in automotive, third-party logistics, food and beverage, and pharmaceutical applications.
The acquisition will combine OTTO’s strong presence in North America with AGILOX’s established customer base in Europe, Rockwell said.
The acquisition also marks an exit for global investment firm Carlyle, which made a majority growth investment in AGILOX in 2021. According to Carlyle, AGILOX has quadrupled its revenue since then and launched a robots-as-a-service (RaaS) offering.
How AGILOX and OTTO Motors compare
Both AGILOX and OTTO Motors develop AMRs that automate material transport. However, there are several differences between their technologies.
OTTO specializes in flatbed AMRs that can be equipped with attachments such as conveyors and lift tables to transport different materials. Its largest AMR, the OTTO 1500, can carry payloads of up to 1,900 kg (4,189 lb.). The company also offers an autonomous forklift called OTTO Lifter.
AGILOX specializes in AMRs with integrated lifting mechanisms for transporting pallets and other loads. Its robots feature omnidirectional drives, enabling them to move sideways and diagonally without turning. This allows the robots to maneuver in tight spaces. AGILOX also has an autonomous forklift.
Rockwell said the technologies are complementary, but it did not disclose how it plans to integrate the two platforms or whether customers will eventually be able to operate AGILOX and OTTO robots as part of the same fleet.
Rockwell continues to expand its robotics business
The AGILOX deal comes three years after Rockwell acquired Clearpath Robotics, including its industrial division, OTTO Motors. Rockwell valued that acquisition at $609 million at the time of purchase.
Founded in 2009 in Ontario, Canada, Clearpath initially developed mobile robots for research applications. It launched OTTO Motors in 2015 to automate material handling in industrial environments.
Rockwell has also invested in robotics beyond AMRs. In March 2025, it made a strategic investment in RightHand Robotics, a Massachusetts-based developer of robotic piece-picking systems for warehouses. Financial terms of that investment were not disclosed. RightHand Robotics combines AI, machine vision, and robotic gripping to pick individual items in fulfillment operations.
