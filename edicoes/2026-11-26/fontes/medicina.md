## Texto extraído

PET/CT imaging provides new insights into type 1 diabetes progression
A novel molecular imaging approach that measures beta-cell mass in the pancreas can offer valuable insights into the progression of type 1 diabetes, according to new research published in the Journal of Nuclear Medicine. The study found that individuals who have had type 1 diabetes for less than seven years have higher total beta-cell mass and function, larger pancreatic volume and lower intrapancreatic fat compared to those who have had the disease for longer. This valuable information, paired with functional tests, has the potential to significantly improve clinical trials of new therapeutic interventions.
Globally, more than 9.5 million people live with type 1 diabetes. In this chronic disease, pancreatic beta cells are destroyed over time, leading to an insulin deficiency and thereby hyperglycemia.
"Not all beta cells will be destroyed in type 1 diabetes, but the exact timing and fate of beta-cell loss are still unknown," said Sevilay Tokgöz, Ph.D., a postdoctoral researcher at Radboud University Medical Center in Nijmegen, the Netherlands. "A better understanding of this process is required to develop therapeutic strategies to halt disease progression."
In the study, Tokgöz and colleagues measured total beta-cell mass using ⁶⁸Ga-NODAGA-exendin-4 PET/CT imaging, an approach that targets GLP-1 receptors that are highly expressed on beta cells. Data from 16 individuals with short-duration type 1 diabetes (less than seven years) and 10 with long-duration type 1 diabetes (more than seven years) were analyzed. Participants underwent ⁶⁸Ga-NODAGA-exendin-4 PET/CT and a mixed-meal tolerance test to determine total beta-cell mass and function, respectively. Intrapancreatic fat deposition and pancreatic volume were derived from CT.
Total beta-cell mass and function were higher in individuals with short-duration type 1 diabetes compared with those with long-duration type 1 diabetes. Larger pancreatic volume and lower intrapancreatic fat deposition were also observed in individuals with short-duration type 1 diabetes compared with the long-duration group. Together, these findings provide new insights into the relationship between beta-cell mass, function and disease duration.
"These findings are highly relevant in regard to the development of therapies aimed at restoring beta-cell function or preserving beta-cell mass," said Tokgöz. "They may aid in more precise estimates of treatment effects and improve the logistics of clinical trials, and in the future could identify individuals who may benefit from therapeutic interventions."
